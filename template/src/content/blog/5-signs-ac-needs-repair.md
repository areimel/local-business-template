## Unusual Noises Coming From Your Unit

If your air conditioner is making grinding, squealing, or banging noises, it's trying to tell you something. These sounds often indicate loose or worn components needing attention before they cause a complete breakdown.

**What to listen for:**
- Grinding or metal-on-metal sounds (possible motor bearing failure)
- Squealing or screeching (belt issues or motor problems)
- Banging or clanking (loose or broken internal components)

## Warm Air Blowing From Vents

When your AC is running but only blowing warm or room-temperature air, several issues could be at play. This is one of the most common complaints homeowners have as temperatures start to climb.

Common causes include low refrigerant levels, a failing compressor, or restricted airflow from a dirty filter. While changing your filter is an easy DIY fix, refrigerant and compressor issues require professional diagnosis.

## Unusually High Energy Bills

A sudden spike in your electricity bill, without a corresponding increase in usage, often points to an AC system that's working harder than it should. As components wear down, your system loses efficiency and runs longer to achieve the same comfort level.

If your bills have jumped 20% or more compared to the same period last year, it's worth having a technician inspect your system before the problem worsens.

## Frequent Cycling On and Off

Your AC should run in relatively consistent cycles. If it's turning on and off every few minutes (short cycling), this puts enormous stress on the compressor and other components, leading to premature failure.

Short cycling can be caused by an oversized system, a refrigerant leak, a faulty thermostat, or electrical issues. A professional can quickly diagnose the root cause.

## Excess Humidity Indoors

Your AC plays a critical role in dehumidification. If you're noticing condensation on windows, a musty smell, or that sticky feeling indoors even when the AC is running, your system may not be performing properly.

This could indicate an aging system that's lost capacity, incorrect sizing, or issues with your ductwork that are allowing humid outside air to infiltrate your conditioned space.
